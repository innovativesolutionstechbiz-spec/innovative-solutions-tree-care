Tree care resources

Learn what to look for

Helpful starting points for observing tree condition and deciding when to seek professional guidance.

Signs worth checking

Watch for large dead branches, new cracks, sudden leaning, soil heaving near the base, or major changes in the canopy. These signs deserve prompt attention.

Pruning thoughtfully

Pruning goals should be clear before cutting. Avoid removing excessive live canopy or making indiscriminate cuts. The right approach depends on species, structure, season, and condition.

After a storm

Keep away from downed power lines and damaged trees under tension. Contact the utility provider for electrical hazards and emergency services when there is immediate danger.

Roots and soil

Compaction, grade changes, trenching, and damage near the root zone can affect tree stability and health. Protect the area around the trunk and visible roots.

Mulch basics

A broad, moderate layer of organic mulch can help conserve moisture. Keep mulch pulled back from direct contact with the trunk; avoid piling it against bark.

When to seek help

Large limbs over structures, significant cracks, hanging branches, and unstable trees can be dangerous. Do not climb or attempt hazardous cutting without proper training and equipment.

This page provides general educational information, not a diagnosis or site-specific safety determination. For urgent hazards, keep people clear and contact the appropriate qualified professionals.
